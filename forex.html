The FX strategy, rule by rule

Same engine as the crypto desk. A 4h impulse leg sets direction and levels; the pullback into its 50% arms the setup; a 15m market-structure shift with three candles in the trade direction is the entry. The 0.886 is the stop. What changes for FX is the filters around it, because currencies move in pips, trade in sessions and close for the weekend.

  1. The leg. Latest confirmed 4h swing high and swing low (3 candles each side). Newest pivot a high → up-leg → long only. Newest a low → down-leg → short only. Trade with the 4h leg, never against it.
  2. Minimum leg size. Skip legs smaller than the pair's floor — EURUSD 40, GBPUSD 50, AUDUSD 30, USDCHF 35, USDJPY 50 pips. A thin leg puts the 0.886 stop inside the spread and noise.
  3. Step 1 — arm. Price trades into the 50% retracement (± 3% of the leg). The first touch after the leg prints arms it.
  4. Step 2 — enter. After the touch, a 15m candle closes beyond the latest 15m swing (above the last swing high for longs, below the last swing low for shorts) and it is the third consecutive candle in the trade direction. Enter at that close — but only if it is still at or deeper than the 0.382. A confirmation that closes nearer the swing extreme leaves TP1 under ~0.75R; let it go.
  5. Session filter. Only take Step 2 inside liquid hours: London 07:00–16:00 and New York 12:00–20:00 UTC (the overlap is best). AUDUSD and USDJPY may also trigger in Asia, 23:00–07:00 UTC. No new entries after 18:00 UTC Friday — never carry a fresh trade into the weekend gap. Avoid the 30 minutes either side of high-impact news for either currency (NFP, CPI, central-bank decisions); this page cannot see the calendar.
  6. Stop. The 0.886 retracement plus a spread buffer (2 pips by default). If price hits it before Step 2 confirms, the setup is dead — wait for the next leg.
  7. Targets. Close a third at the swing extreme (the 0.0) and move the stop to break-even; a third at the −0.272; let the last third run to the −0.618. From the 50% that is roughly 1.3R / 2R / 2.9R before the buffer.
  8. Size. One stop-loss budget shared by the five pairs — $2,000 on a $100,000 account split five ways is $400 a trade, so all five stopping out together costs 2%. Each trade is sized so its stop loses exactly its share: lots = risk $ ÷ (stop distance × 100,000 ÷ quote rate). That is $10 a pip per lot on EURUSD, GBPUSD and AUDUSD; on USDCHF and USDJPY a pip is worth 10 CHF / 1,000 JPY per lot, converted at the stop price. Lots round down to 0.01 so you never exceed the risk.
  9. One trade per leg. A new 4h swing extreme resets the setup; a higher low inside an up-leg (or a lower high inside a down-leg) is that leg's pullback and does not. If price runs back past the swing extreme without a valid Step 2, it is a miss — do not chase.
  10. Correlation. EURUSD and GBPUSD (and USDCHF, inverted) often trigger together — they are mostly the same USD bet. The split already caps that at the total budget; if three of them line up the same way, taking only the cleanest one or two keeps you from losing 1.2% on what is one idea.

The ladder is the alternative entry. Instead of waiting for the 15m confirmation, rest limit orders from the 0.382 to the 0.618 with sizes ramping deeper, stop at the 0.886, targets spread across the −0.618 → −1.0 — exactly the crypto Fib Ladder, but sized so the whole ladder filled loses that pair's share of the budget at the stop, not by margin. It fills more often and earns more per win, and it is wrong more often because nothing has confirmed the turn.
