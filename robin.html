🐦 The Robin

How the Robin works

The six likeliest props across every sport on the slate — baseball hits, basketball points, rebounds, assists and threes, football passing, receiving and rushing yards — bet as a round robin: every 2-leg parlay between them, every 3-leg parlay, and so on up to all six.

A round robin forgives a miss. One cold leg costs only the tickets it is on; with six legs, by 2s is fifteen tickets and a single miss still leaves ten of them alive. The legs come from different games wherever the slate allows, because a round robin only prices honestly when its legs are independent.

Chosen from the same ranked pool as the Ladder and the Dub, at the same lock — between −200 and −350, and only props our model rates within a few points of their price — each leg at its live Kalshi price. How much to bet: the Robin has its own bankroll and plays the whole round robin — every 2-, 3-, 4-, 5- and 6-leg ticket, 57 with six legs. Each ticket is your Robin balance divided by 570, so the bankroll always covers ten full days: $1 a ticket at $570, $1.22 at $694.52. It moves with the balance, up after a winning day and down after a losing one. Every row below prices one size at those prices: what each ticket costs, what an average ticket pays, how many should cash, and whether that adds up to more than you put in.
